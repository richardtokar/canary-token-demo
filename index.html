What just happened

That receipt was a trap called a canary token.

Hidden inside the file is a tiny, invisible picture. When Word opens the file, it grabs that picture from the internet. That one request tells the owner: someone just opened me.

Why “canary”? 🐤

Coal miners used to bring canaries underground. The birds got sick from poisonous gas before people did, so a canary in trouble meant get out now. British mines used real canaries until 1986.

A canary token is the same idea for computers: an early warning. Nobody should ever touch it, so if anyone does, something is wrong.

Security pros hide them everywhere: fake passwords in code, fake files on servers, fake rows in databases. They also call them honeytokens, because they’re bait. Hackers can’t tell which secrets are real, so every “find” is a risk for them.
